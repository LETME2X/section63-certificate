Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 — The Schedule, see section 63(4)(c)

Certificate for an electronic record

Fills the Schedule form (Part A and Part B), computes the hash value of the electronic record on this computer, and produces the certificate and the hash report as Word documents. Also checks a hash value stated by someone else against a file. Nothing you enter or drop here leaves this page.

Which law governs your proceeding? This is the form prescribed by the Schedule to the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023, in force from 1 July 2024. Under section 170 of the Adhiniyam, matters pending before that date may continue under the Indian Evidence Act, 1872, where section 65B applies and no form is prescribed. Confirm which law applies before using this certificate.

The form below stays in English; the documents and the quoted Schedule lines follow this choice.

1. Electronic record(s) and hash value

“I state that the HASH value/s of the electronic/digital record/s is ___, obtained through the following algorithm:— ☐ SHA1: ☐ SHA256: ☐ MD5: ☐ Other (Legally acceptable standard) (Hash report to be enclosed with the certificate)”

Drop the file(s) that constitute the electronic record here, or click to choose. Drop the exact file you will produce to the court; the hash is computed on it as it is now.

Do not open and re-save an exported file after hashing; the hash will change. Very large files may take some time.

3. Part B — to be filled by the Expert

“The produced electronic record/output of the digital record are obtained from the following device/digital record source (tick mark):— … I state that the HASH value/s of the electronic/digital record/s is ___ … (Name, designation and signature)”

The device particulars, source ticks, hash values and algorithm in Part B are printed from Part A above. The expert should verify them before signing. Who may sign Part B is an open question of law — see decision 4 in section 6. Nothing here is copied from the Part A signatory.

4. Produce the documents

Blank fields are printed as blank lines for completion by hand. The certificate is a Word file in the Schedule's wording; check it against the Gazette text before filing. Independently verify a hash with certutil -hashfile <file> SHA256 (Windows) or shasum -a 256 <file> (macOS/Linux).

5. Check a hash value stated by someone else

When the other side, or the police, files a certificate with a hash value, drop the file they produced and paste the value from their certificate. The page recomputes the hash and tells you whether it is the same file. The algorithm is read from the length of the value: 64 characters SHA256, 40 SHA1, 32 MD5.

Drop the file that was produced with that certificate, or click to choose.

6. Decisions this page does not make

Drafted from the sources linked. Not legal advice. For counsel's review before reliance.

  1. Is a certificate required at all? Section 63 applies to a “computer output” — information from an electronic record that is printed, stored, recorded or copied — proved without producing the original. Section 57, Explanation 5, treats an electronic record produced from proper custody as primary evidence unless it is disputed. In Arjun Panditrao Khotkar v. Kailash Kushanrao Gorantyal, (2020) 7 SCC 1, para 34, the Supreme Court said the certificate is unnecessary where the original device is produced by its owner, who proves that the device is owned or operated by him; the Himachal Pradesh High Court applied this under the Adhiniyam in Nirmal Malakar v. Larisa Enterprises (1 June 2026), and the Patna High Court in Jyoti Rani v. Nishant Kumar (6 August 2026) set aside a divorce decree because WhatsApp messages had been exhibited without the certificate, holding that oral evidence cannot cure the omission. Whether you are producing the original or a copy is your decision. Sources: Act text, official Gazette PDF (MHA); Nirmal Malakar (HP HC), Indian Kanoon; Jyoti Rani (Patna HC), LiveLaw.
  2. Which law governs the proceeding? The Adhiniyam came into force on 1 July 2024. Section 170 saves the Indian Evidence Act, 1872 for matters pending before that date, where section 65B applies and no form of certificate is prescribed. This page produces only the Schedule form under the Adhiniyam. Source: Act text, official Gazette PDF (MHA).
  3. Who signs Part A? Section 63(4) requires signature by a person in charge of the computer or communication device or the management of the relevant activities, whichever is appropriate. If the record came from a device you do not control — a service provider's server, another person's DVR — the appropriate signatory may be its custodian rather than you. The Andhra Pradesh High Court (C.R.P. No. 1239 of 2024, decided May 2026, a section 65B matter) set aside a trial court order that had assumed only a “proper authority” could certify, holding that a person in lawful control of the device may do so if the statutory contents are present. Source: LiveLaw report, 13 May 2026.
  4. Who signs Part B? Section 63(4) adds the words “and an expert”. Section 39(2) treats an Examiner of Electronic Evidence notified under section 79A of the Information Technology Act, 2000 as an expert; few have been notified (MeitY added six laboratories on 9 July 2026). In Pune Bar Association v. Union of India, W.P.(C) No. 599 of 2026 (22 May 2026), the Supreme Court observed that a person having special skill and expertise in computer science and cyber forensics may sign Part B if the court is satisfied on unimpeachable material, held that the Madras High Court's contrary view is not binding precedent, and expressly kept the question of law open. This page does not pre-fill Part B from Part A. Sources: SC order; MeitY notification report.
  5. What exactly is hashed? The Schedule asks for the hash of “the electronic/digital record/s”. This page hashes the file you drop, as it is at that moment. Commentators differ on whether the original record, the copy produced to the court, or both should be hashed. If you produce a printout, the paper has no hash; state which file the hash refers to. Source: Saji Koduvath, commentary.
  6. When must the certificate be filed? Section 63(4) says the certificate is to be submitted along with the electronic record at each instance where it is being submitted for admission. Under section 65B the Supreme Court in Arjun Panditrao allowed later production in some circumstances. Whether that flexibility carries into the Adhiniyam is for counsel to assess. Source: Act text, official Gazette PDF (MHA); (2020) 7 SCC 1.
  7. Does a Family Court need the certificate at all? Section 14 of the Family Courts Act, 1984 allows a Family Court to receive any report, statement, document, information or matter that may assist it, whether or not it would otherwise be admissible under the law of evidence. The Bombay High Court (AMB v. SB, 2026:BHC-NAG:5200-DB, April 2026) treated section 65B objections as inapplicable in family proceedings on that basis. The Patna High Court, four months later in Jyoti Rani, applied the certificate requirement strictly in a matrimonial appeal. Which line your Family Court follows is for counsel; the safer course is usually to file the certificate anyway. Sources: AMB v. SB (Bombay HC), Verdictum; Jyoti Rani (Patna HC), LiveLaw.
