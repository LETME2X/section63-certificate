Certificate under section 63(4)(c), Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023

Fills the Schedule form (Part A and Part B), computes the hash value of the electronic record on this computer, and produces the certificate and the hash report as Word documents. Nothing you enter or drop here leaves this page.

Which law governs your proceeding? This is the form prescribed by the Schedule to the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023, in force from 1 July 2024. Under section 170 of the Adhiniyam, matters pending before that date may continue under the Indian Evidence Act, 1872, where section 65B applies and no form is prescribed. Confirm which law applies before using this certificate.

Drop the file(s) that constitute the electronic record here, or click to choose. Drop the exact file you will produce to the court; the hash is computed on it as it is now.

Do not open and re-save an exported file after hashing; the hash will change. Very large files may take some time.

Blank fields are printed as blank lines for completion by hand. The certificate is a Word file in the Schedule's wording; check it against the Gazette text before filing. Independently verify a hash with certutil -hashfile <file> SHA256 (Windows) or shasum -a 256 <file> (macOS/Linux).

5. Decisions this page does not make

Drafted from the sources linked. Not legal advice. For counsel's review before reliance.

  1. Is a certificate required at all? Section 63 applies to a “computer output” — information from an electronic record that is printed, stored, recorded or copied — proved without producing the original. Section 57, Explanation 5, treats an electronic record produced from proper custody as primary evidence unless it is disputed. In Arjun Panditrao Khotkar v. Kailash Kushanrao Gorantyal, (2020) 7 SCC 1, para 34, the Supreme Court said the certificate is unnecessary where the original device is produced by its owner, who proves that the device is owned or operated by him; the Himachal Pradesh High Court applied this under the Adhiniyam in Nirmal Malakar v. Larisa Enterprises (1 June 2026). Whether you are producing the original or a copy is your decision. Sources: Act text, India Code; Nirmal Malakar (HP HC).
  2. Which law governs the proceeding? The Adhiniyam came into force on 1 July 2024. Section 170 saves the Indian Evidence Act, 1872 for matters pending before that date, where section 65B applies and no form of certificate is prescribed. This page produces only the Schedule form under the Adhiniyam. Source: Act text, India Code.
  3. Who signs Part A? Section 63(4) requires signature by a person in charge of the computer or communication device or the management of the relevant activities, whichever is appropriate. If the record came from a device you do not control — a service provider's server, another person's DVR — the appropriate signatory may be its custodian rather than you. The Andhra Pradesh High Court (C.R.P. No. 1239 of 2024, decided May 2026, a section 65B matter) set aside a trial court order that had assumed only a “proper authority” could certify, holding that a person in lawful control of the device may do so if the statutory contents are present. Source: LiveLaw report, 13 May 2026.
  4. Who signs Part B? Section 63(4) adds the words “and an expert”. Section 39(2) treats an Examiner of Electronic Evidence notified under section 79A of the Information Technology Act, 2000 as an expert; few have been notified (MeitY added six laboratories on 9 July 2026). In Pune Bar Association v. Union of India, W.P.(C) No. 599 of 2026 (22 May 2026), the Supreme Court observed that a person having special skill and expertise in computer science and cyber forensics may sign Part B if the court is satisfied on unimpeachable material, held that the Madras High Court's contrary view is not binding precedent, and expressly kept the question of law open. This page does not pre-fill Part B from Part A. Sources: SC order; MeitY notification report.
  5. What exactly is hashed? The Schedule asks for the hash of “the electronic/digital record/s”. This page hashes the file you drop, as it is at that moment. Commentators differ on whether the original record, the copy produced to the court, or both should be hashed. If you produce a printout, the paper has no hash; state which file the hash refers to. Source: Saji Koduvath, commentary.
  6. When must the certificate be filed? Section 63(4) says the certificate is to be submitted along with the electronic record at each instance where it is being submitted for admission. Under section 65B the Supreme Court in Arjun Panditrao allowed later production in some circumstances. Whether that flexibility carries into the Adhiniyam is for counsel to assess. Source: Act text, India Code; (2020) 7 SCC 1.
